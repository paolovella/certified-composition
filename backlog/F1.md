## Plan

Filed on the user's word (2026-10-29): "later passes will get slower and slower"; item F1 of [FOUNDATION-2026-10-29](../research/2026-10/FOUNDATION-2026-10-29.md). A sub-item
of P0-06, split out so P0-06 can close on its own acceptance.

**The gap.**
- `tools/ci.sh` records only the total time in its receipt: 954 s for `ph1-p1-ci-full`, and
  1037 s and 1018 s for the two PH1 runs before it. Nothing says which step costs what, so
  growth is invisible until the total hurts.
- The sandboxing tools (`mutants.py` and the registry, mutant, gate, comparator, ring0-lock and
  warden witnesses) already reuse the main tree's build output: each copies the packages'
  `.lake` into its sandbox. (A correction of what was said in session on 2026-10-29:
  ~~the others rebuild from scratch~~. Every one copies `.lake`.) Their cost is the copy and
  the rebuild of whatever their scenario changed.
- The rule mutants dominate: that step is most of the log (lines 71–394 of 406 in
  `ph1-p1-ci-full.log`). `mutants.py` copies all three packages, build output included, once
  per mutant (`copytree` at `tools/mutants.py:90-93`), then rebuilds every module downstream
  of the mutated file. Both costs grow with the packages: the copy with their size, the
  rebuild with how much depends on a checker. Every pass that imports a checker module adds
  to the rebuild of each of that module's mutants.

**Steps.**
0. **A quick tier.** `ci.sh --quick` builds only the packages a change touches (by `git diff`
   against the branch base), then runs check-claims, the axiom manifest check and the guard
   self-test. It prints every full-CI step it skipped as `DEFERRED: quick tier`, so it never
   skips silently, and it never counts as the green a merge needs.
1. **Measure.** `ci.sh` writes `tools/receipts/<job>.steps`: one line per step, with the step
   name, its seconds and its status. It prints a slowest-first summary at the end.
2. **Baseline and variance.** Two identical full runs on the WSL box. Record per-step times
   and their spread in this item. The kill rule is judged here.
3. **Ratchet.** `tools/ci-budget.tsv` gives each step a budget: its baseline times a slack
   factor fixed in step 2. A step over budget fails CI and is named. The budget may be raised
   only in a change that says why (a gate data file, like `checkers.lock`). A witness slows a
   step artificially and shows the ratchet firing, and an unchanged run shows it passing.
4. **Mutants: cheaper sandboxes.** Today each mutant runs a full `lake build` of
   `conf-typing` (`tools/mutants.py:98`), 4 at a time (`--jobs 4`). Copy the unchanged
   packages with reflinks or hard links instead of a full `copytree`. Then measure, against
   the step-2 baseline, whether a higher `--jobs` helps on this box.
5. **Mutants: build only what can kill (option, judged on the numbers).** Building only the
   mutated module and the modules holding its rule's witnesses would make each mutant's
   cost independent of later passes. But it changes what "killed" means. Today any
   downstream proof that breaks kills a mutant; under this change only the witnesses could.
   That is a stricter test, and mutants that survive it are findings. Adopt it only with the
   user's decision, and never by weakening `mutants.expected` to fit.

The gate's own sandbox (`base-gate.sh`) is left alone throughout, because it must not trust
the PR's build output.

**Out of scope.** Kernel evaluation time of the compiler pipeline. Fixed structurally on
`ph1/spine` (`PassCert.seq` compiles each stage once); PH5 measures it.
