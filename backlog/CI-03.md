## Plan

**The gap, checked.** `tools/witnesses.expected` lists 39 checkers, all `ConfTyping.`, and none from `LCore`. `tools/mutants.py` reads its checkers from it. So CI mutates and registers only `conf-typing`.

L's checkers live in `l-core`: `sliceCheck`, `ctCheck`, `ctOutCheck`, `counted`, the C front end, the lowering's `okC`, and the machine model. Their mutants are hand-run scripts that no CI step runs:

| Script | Mutants | Time on the WSL box |
|---|---|---|
| `research/2026-11/slice2_mut.py` | 26 | 38 s |
| `research/2026-11/slice2_low_mut.py` | 68 | 670 s |
| `research/2026-11/ctout_mut.py` | 4 | 2 s |
| `research/2026-11/cfront_mut.py` | 18 | not timed here |
| `research/2026-11/boundr_mut.py`, `slice_mut.py` | (VS-1, BOUND-01) | not timed here |
| `l-core/test/rv_diff.py --mutants` | 47 | 120 s |

The QEMU comparisons are not CI steps either (`rv_diff.py`, `image2_diff.py`, `bound_diff.py`, `verify16_diff.py`, `out_diff.py`). That is VS-1's proposal 3, still open.

So an agent could weaken an `l-core` checker rule, keep every proof building by also weakening a witness, and CI would stay green.

**Proposed, for the user's key.**
- The fast scripts become CI steps, each with a baseline that must build: `slice2_mut`, `ctout_mut`, `cfront_mut`, `rv_diff --mutants`.
- The slow lowering script runs on the heavy runner (`P0-06`, `F7`).
- Every script gets the baseline `ctout_mut.py` now has. Its first version had none, and would have counted every mutant as killed if the copy failed for another reason (`RC-02` step 4).
- The registry extends to `LCore` checkers.
- Each new step gets a refused witness.
