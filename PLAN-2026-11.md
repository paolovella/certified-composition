## 2. What 1.0 is

1. **One language.** The grown slice language is L. The thirteen `UCmd`-family languages move to `museum/`, built and frozen. Guard: `inductive .*Cmd` outside `museum/` counts 1.
2. **One theorem per program.** For a program the unified checker accepts, one theorem gives, on its literal image in `rvLang`, by transfer alone:

   | Row | Property | Shape on bytes | Today |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1, 2, 13 | no secret leak by output, time, address or termination | `Cert.rel`, `Cert.relView` | on bytes |
   | 10 | a budget on running cost | `Cert.bound`, `Cert.boundR` | on bytes |
   | 7 | permission is checked before each read; tenants are isolated | `Cert.trace` over `Ev.check` and `Ev.read`; `Cert.relView` at a tenant's observer | on `UCmd` |
   | 4 | only declared values are released | `Cert.rel` at the reduced partition, with `Ev.release` | on `UCmd`, whole variables |
   | 3, 14 | untrusted input cannot change a query's or a path's structure | `Cert.trace` per sink over `Ev.emit` | on `UCmd`, loop-free |
   | 6 | no use after free, double free or wild access; no leak on finished runs | source safety and the pass certificates | on `UCmd` |
   | 9 | no leak under branch misprediction | `Cert.rel` on a speculating RV64 model | on `UCmd`, by a window |
   | 12 | passes compose, and components compose | `PassCert.seq`; a seam theorem | passes on bytes; components on `UCmd` |

   Rows 5 (differential privacy), 8 (races) and 11 (crypto assumptions) stay outside the certificate shapes, and the map says so.
3. **The flagship.** One C request handler. It checks a tag in constant time, asks permission before reading a resource, builds an SQL query and a file path from untrusted input, logs, allocates and frees, and releases one declared value, within a stated budget. Its ELF runs under QEMU and matches the model and clang. Each row has a broken variant that the checker refuses and whose image misbehaves.
4. **The blind measure.** The 40 tasks of `conf-typing/corpus/blind`, ported to L: how many are accepted, refused, or not expressible, reported as measured.
5. **The engine.** `AUTONOMY` is `cells-only`, the gates are tested by planted failures, and agents have merged cells on green without the user.
6. **The record.**
   - `VULN-COVERAGE` §3.0 names, for each row, the theorem on bytes or a correction of record.
   - The 1.0 theorems are in the frozen statements, and Ring 0 v1 is locked with the user's key.
   - `bash tools/ci.sh` is green locally and in Actions, and route 2's external kernels agree.

**Every claim stays relative to its trusted base**, which the release states:
- Lean's kernel and the external kernels;
- the RV64 model, as compared with QEMU;
- the C reading, as tested against clang;
- the hardware contract for time and speculation, which no test here can check;
- the permission monitor, as an oracle.

## 7. How each item is run

As [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) says: one item, its plan written before code, a branch, long jobs through `tools/run-job.sh`,
`bash tools/ci.sh` green, a commit, `tools/render-state.sh`. Each item carries an acceptance and a kill rule. Patterns to reuse:

- **A new instruction:** `Slice/Rv.lean` (encode, decode, step), a case in `test/RvCases.lean`, and broken models in `test/rv_diff.py`.
- **A new construct:** an arm in `Slice/Source.lean`, `Slots.lean`, `Lower.lean` and their `Slice2` counterparts, as `shl` was added beside `shr`. Mutants in `research/2026-11/slice2_low_mut.py` and `cfront_mut.py`.
- **A new certificate:** a checker at the source, and its transfer through `slice_carries` and `pipeline_comp` with no pass lines, as `ctCheck` was.
- **A C reading:** a READING note that states the trust assumption, tested against clang, as [`W32-01-READING`](research/2026-11/W32-01-READING.md) and `test/w32_diff.py`.
- **The rules to start from** are on `UCmd`, in `conf-typing/ConfTyping/`: `Mediation`, `TenantL`, `ReleaseL`, `InjectionL`, `PathL`, `MemCert`, `LeakL`, `SpecL`, `SeamL`. Their witnesses come with them.

## 8. How the plan is checked

- **The schedule is data** (`PLAN-11` part 2). `tools/milestones.tsv` is the source, `STATE.md` shows the next milestone and the days left, and `check-claims` (s) fails when a milestone is still `open` more than 7 days after its date. The three ways out are the ones §5 names, and each leaves a record:
  - **done:** the row's status becomes `done`.
  - **moved:** the row and §3's table get the new date, the row's fourth column keeps the date it had, and §9 gets a line with the words `<id> moved from <earlier date>` and the reason.
  - **cut:** the row's status becomes `cut`, and §9 gets a line with the words `<id> cut`.
- **Each of the three is a change to a file under `tools/`, so it needs the user's key**, and the base gate reads BASE's copy of the file and of the rule. A branch cannot mark its own milestone done.
- **What the rule cannot see:** a row marked `done` that is not (the next bullet is the test, and a person applies it), and a date changed in the file and the table together with nothing kept in the fourth column. Both are Ring 0 edits, so the user sees them.
- **What the rule costs:** it depends on the day it runs. Eight days after a missed date, `check-claims` is red on every branch, whatever that branch changed, until the user merges one of the three records. `MILESTONES_TODAY=YYYY-MM-DD` replays the rule at another date.
- **Until part 2 is merged with the user's key**, a branch that does not contain it checks nothing, and a missed date is caught only by the Friday line below.
- **A milestone is met** only when its items are `done` against their acceptance, with receipts under `tools/receipts/`, and `bash tools/ci.sh`, `tools/check-claims.sh` and `tools/guard-selftest.sh` are green.
- **Each Friday**, the session on Track A adds a line to §9: the milestone dates against the actual ones, the slack used, the cuts applied.
- **1.0 itself:** the flagship's ELF under QEMU against the model and clang; each broken variant refused, and misbehaving; the engine's planted failures caught; route 2's kernels agreeing on the frozen statements.
