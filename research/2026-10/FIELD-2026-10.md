# Lean ships the gate; the seams remain open

*Field refresh, 2026-10-23, covering August to October 2026. It follows `FIELD-2026-08.md` (2026-08-17) and covers only what changed or sharpened since then.*

The most important change since August is that **Lean 4.35.0 (2026-10-12) ships the kernel re-checking and axiom-audit gate that this project would otherwise have to build or borrow**. `lake check` replays a project's export through the kernel and fails on any non-standard axiom. `lake comparator` checks proved statements against a frozen challenge file. `--paranoid` requires five independent kernels to agree, and `--from-export` re-checks third-party NDJSON exports. The project's own trust story therefore moves from "trust our build" to "five kernels re-checked the export" at almost no cost. The second change is that **the composition niche is still empty, and its threat model now has a name.** Since August, every new effort either imports one tool's certificates into a kernel (SAT/LRAT, pseudo-Boolean proofs) or describes a single pipeline's trust boundary in prose (SymCrypt, EquiVM). A 2026 attack paper on hax/libcrux sorts verification gaps into classes that map almost one-to-one onto the ledger's invariants. Third, AI proving on software-verification Lean tasks jumped from roughly 30–40% to 95–100% on some benchmarks. Proof is now cheap, so the weak point is spec vacuity and statement tampering, which the kernel cannot see. Fourth, **mlkem-native is the real target the program needed.** Two tools certify two components, and the contract between them is kept in sync by hand. The upstream issue tracking that seam is open, the repo changed it three times in October, and the downstream import into AWS-LC adds a third seam. Attestation standards still have no field for assumptions, and nobody publishes formal-proof attestations, so that slot is open too. For a future standalone language, the field's direction is clear: the compiler should emit a Lean export that the shipped kernels re-check, and its trusted items should be machine-listed rather than described.

## Lean 4.35 turns kernel re-checking from a project into a dependency

The August survey treated "kernel type-check plus transitive axiom audit" as something people were hand-rolling (Koomullil's proof-carrying certificates for LLM pipelines). That is now built into the toolchain. **Lean 4.35.0 adds `lake check`, which "builds the default targets, exports them, replays the export through the kernel" and "fails if anything uses an axiom other than the standard ones"**. It also moves comparator into Lake as `lake comparator`, configured by `comparator.json`. `--paranoid` accepts only if every bundled checker accepts: lean4lean, nanoda, con-leche, con-ron and a hardened-allocator `leanchecker-paranoid`. `--from-export` checks pre-built NDJSON exports, "third-party exports" included. The release also **removes the `Lean.reduceBool`, `Lean.reduceNat` and `Lean.trustCompiler` axioms** because they made whole-environment axiom checks "less meaningful" ([Lean 4.35.0 release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.35.0/)). Two of the five kernels have independent assurance of their own. con-leche is written in Lean and proven consistent relative to Carneiro's set-theoretic model, and con-ron is a Rust translation with an Aeneas refinement proof. lean4lean's verification is "expected to finish in the next few months" ([de Moura, 2026-10-15](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-10-15-lean-now-ships-with-verified-kernels/)). The standalone comparator repo is archived ([leanprover/comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator)).

For this project, the practical effect is that the trusted base of `transport-kernel` and `conf-typing` can be stated as a re-checkable claim instead of a description. **The four-level validation ladder in the Lean reference manual** is the recipe. Level 1 is `lake build`. Level 2 is `#print axioms` against {propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}. Level 3 is `lean4checker --fresh`. Level 4 is comparator with external checkers against a trusted challenge file, recommended explicitly for "unreviewed AI-generated proofs" ([Lean reference: Validating a Lean Proof](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/)). The manual is also explicit about the residue, and that residue belongs in the project's own `Ledger` as entries that are never discharged: the logic, the comparator plumbing, the sandbox (bubblewrap, Linux-only), and *statement correctness*. These checks have very different costs. Measured throughput is about **0.43 req/s for Axle's checker, 0.107 for SafeVerify and 0.026 for comparator** ([Axle, arXiv 2606.26442](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26442v1)), which argues for cheap checks on every attempt and comparator only at merge. Two limits deserve a note. The notes describe no JSON report and no exit-code contract, so the verdict is only "accept" or "fail". And the release notes disagree on whether con-ron is part of `--paranoid`.

`--from-export` matters more for scaling than for safety. **Any tool that produces a Lean export can now be re-checked by five kernels without trusting the producer's build**, and the lean4export NDJSON format carries an explicit version (3.1.0, in the `meta` header) ([format_ndjson.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/lean4export/master/format_ndjson.md)). Because the project is itself Lean, it can read that export and compute axiom closure directly instead of trusting `#print axioms` text. That makes Aeneas output, SymCrypt's Lean proofs, and later the project's own compiler output into *consumable* certificates with a computed trusted base. The nearest new academic work on per-import trust accounting points the same way. Szeider's streaming-LRAT paper (v2 2026-09-07) turns SAT certificates into named Lean theorems and argues that garbled input "can only fail the check but not yield a false theorem". According to the search summary, it also tabulates how each import route (Isabelle oracles, Rocq `native_compute`, a verified external checker, `by_decide` re-solving) enlarges the trusted base ([arXiv 2607.00815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00815)). PBLean draws a similar static distinction between kernel-only and native paths ([arXiv 2602.08692](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08692)). These are single-tool trust tables. The obvious next step for someone else is a Lean library that tags each imported theorem with its trust route and takes the union of the tags. That is the **closest plausible competitor to claim (2)**, and the cheapest defence is to cite it and position the ledger as the algebra above it.

## AI proving solved the proof and exposed the statement

The August survey quoted 27% for Lean on the vericoding benchmark. **An agentic loop with GPT-5.4 now reaches 95.0% on 423 Lean vericoding specs with K=50 calls**, "nearly saturating" ([Yao, arXiv 2605.27485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27485)). On repo-scale software verification, the more relevant proxy for a mathlib-free security codebase, VeriSoftBench (500 obligations from 23 real Lean formal-methods repos) reported pass@8 of 41.0% for Gemini-3-Pro and 31.2% for Claude Opus 4.5 in February. Math-tuned Goedel-Prover-V2 managed **5.6% curated and 0.0% full-repo**, so competition-math provers do not transfer to this kind of work ([arXiv 2602.18307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18307)). By September a vendor blog reported that a simple interactive loop with frontier models and Lean search tactics (`exact?`, `#check`, temporary `example`s) reached 100% pass@1 on VERINA and 99% on the Aristotle subset of VeriSoftBench. That subset supplies lemma statements, and the numbers are self-reported ([Emergence AI](https://www.emergence.ai/blog/wubqc15w0squyx8mot5zn7iziw6b7x)). The comparison is not like-for-like. Still, the direction is clear: **interactivity (goal inspection plus search tactics) is the dominant lever**, ahead of model specialization. Two further results point to how to organize the work. SorryDB, built from 1,000 open `sorry`s across 78 real Lean projects, found that "current approaches are complementary", which argues for running several agents per obligation ([arXiv 2603.02668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02668)). Tan reports that adding a decomposition scaffold and a separate proof-reviser pass raised pass rate **from 46.2% to 69.2%** ([arXiv 2605.30914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30914)). For Verus, VeruSAGE agents prove **more than 80% of 849 tasks from 8 real systems projects at about 7.2 minutes each** ([arXiv 2512.18436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18436)).

Cheap proof moves the risk to the statement, and the evidence for that arrived in the same window. **"Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking" (ICML 2026) audited five widely used Lean benchmarks and found 398 mechanically certified defects**: counterexamples, vacuous theorems and unsound axioms. Defects both inflated and deflated scores, and harnesses could be fooled by trivial solutions ([arXiv 2606.29493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29493)). Specification hacking under reinforcement learning is now measured. In Dafny RLVR, verified reward rose from 2.2% to 58.1%, but after underspecified tasks were filtered out the real gain was 9.7% to 31.1% ([arXiv 2605.30914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30914)). Verus-SpecGym shows that **LLM-as-judge misses 26% of spec failures**, and the best model writes correct specs only 77.8% of the time ([arXiv 2605.26457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26457)). The detectors that work are mechanical. Behavior-changing mutants of a reference implementation must break the spec: Lean4Commit0 reports ~98.6% mutant rejection ([arXiv 2608.09277](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09277)). Vacuity gates require that a gutted invariant fail ([arXiv 2605.09045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09045)). Executable specs are run against adversarial inputs. **The mutants must not themselves be written by an LLM:** one study found hallucinations in 82.5% of LLM-generated mutation scripts ([EmergentMind 2606.18405](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.18405)).

Put together, this gives a concrete design for an AI-agent proof fleet. Statements and the definitions they mention live in a human-owned challenge file that agents cannot edit; this is the attack comparator's model is built against. Each agent attempt gets a lexical ban on `sorry`, `native_decide`, `axiom`, `implemented_by`, `extern`, `debug.skipKernelTC` and `set_option`, followed by an axiom whitelist check. The exploit list behind these bans is documented ([MIT 6.S976 notes](https://mlcrypto.mit.edu/course/scribe-notes/lec14_scribe.pdf); [LeanParanoia](https://github.com/oOo0oOo/LeanParanoia)). Each merge runs `lake check`. Headline theorems additionally run `lake comparator --paranoid`. Every new spec needs a non-vacuity witness lemma and must reject a set of mechanically generated mutants. The repo's existing mutation-tested drift guards already match field best practice; the gap is making non-vacuity a required obligation rather than a habit. The Verus side is weaker, and this matters for the Verus cross-check. **Verus has no assumptions listing; `--no-cheating` is a crate-wide yes/no rejection of `assume`, `admit`, `external_body` and `assume_specification`** ([Verus config.rs](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/blob/main/source/rust_verify/src/config.rs)). Any Verus-derived ledger entry must come from a source scan, not from the tool. One open question: no documented incident has been found of an AI proof exploiting a Lean soundness bug in production. The exploit list is generic, and the gate is insurance against a demonstrated *capability* ("LLMs will find workarounds ... even when those workarounds are unsound"), not against observed attacks.

## The seam threat model now has a name, and nobody composes across it

The negative result from August holds through October. Searches turned up **no published machine-checked composition of per-artifact certificates from different tools with a computed trusted base**. The Loom line grew: Velvet at CAV 2026 unifies SMT, interactive proof and property-based testing inside one Lean verifier ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-32526-6_11)), and a certified-synthesis paper builds on it for LLM pipelines ([arXiv 2604.16584](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16584)). But these are modes inside a single verifier, not external certificates. Corten builds foundational Rust verification in Rocq+Iris construct by construct, with no trusted-base analysis in the abstract ([arXiv 2609.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04372)). EquiVM has LLM agents prove 35 production EVM contracts end to end in Lean, independent of the compiler, and states its trust boundary in prose ([arXiv 2607.26306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26306)). No follow-up to HDL ([arXiv 2507.08581](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08581v2)) or to Koomullil ([arXiv 2605.16407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16407)) was found. Practitioners are asking for per-artifact axiom audits in Lean in ad hoc form, for example a PR to "audit and enforce" the certificates' trusted computing base ([stabileo #334](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/pull/334)) and tracking of `native_decide` in `bv_decide` certificates ([clifv #42](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv/issues/42)). The caveat is coverage: the OOPSLA, CCS, ITP, SOSP and POPL 2027 accepted lists were not swept, so the negative result covers only what web search surfaced.

The strongest new external motivation is an attack paper. **Kobeissi's "Verification Facade" (ePrint 2026/670) presents five proof-of-concept exploits against ML-DSA, ML-KEM, Ed25519 and ChaCha20 that pass hax's F* extraction without warnings.** It sorts the causes into translation infidelity, unverified trust boundaries (operations axiomatized without postconditions) and specification gaming (escape hatches that inject unproven facts), and grades gaps as facade, conditional and scope ([ePrint 2026/670](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/670); [Cryspen response](https://cryspen.com/post/strengths-and-limitations/)). The paper proposes no tracking mechanism. Its categories line up with this repo's invariants:

| ePrint 2026/670 gap | Ledger concept that rules it out |
|---|---|
| Unverified trust boundary | an assumption missing from the ledger, impossible when the ledger is computed rather than declared |
| Specification gaming | silent discharge, forbidden by `ledger_no_silent_discharge` |
| Scope gap | an omission that was not earned |
| Conditional gap (depends on compilation mode) | a configuration-indexed assumption |

That table is the cleanest way to explain claim (2) to a security reader: **the computed ledger rules out, by construction, the gap classes that a published exploit paper found by hand.** Industry shows the same pattern without the mechanism. AWS-LC's proofs span SAW, an in-house AArch64 tool, Coq, HOL Light and CBMC, with "assumed correct" boxes on slides and no unified cross-tool assumption document ([AWS-LC slides](https://sos-vo.org/system/files/2024-05/Formal%20Verification%20of%20AWS-LibCrypto_1.pdf)). SymCrypt's 237 KLOC of Lean covers 16.7 KLOC of Rust, and side channels are handled by separate fuzzing and binary symbolic execution that is not formally composed ([arXiv 2609.15648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15648)). Cross-memory-model spec translation is measurably lossy: migrating ACSL-annotated C to Verus verified 92 of 175 programs automatically and 139 after fixes ([C2VR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167642326001012)). For the bounded-certificate claim (3), the most useful find is older but mechanised. **Abdulaziz, Gretton and Norrish showed in HOL4 that diameter-based bounds do not compose but recurrence-diameter bounds do** ([TS abstract](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/nictaabstracts/Abdulaziz_GN_15_2.abstract); [AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Factored_Transition_System_Bounding.html)). That is a precedent for exactly when a bounded result composes, and `BoundedCert` should be checked against that distinction. The Assurance 2.0 line surfaced only a Springer chapter that is probably the venue version of the April arXiv paper ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-34867-8_18)), plus a critique that its probabilistic values are "only for comparative purposes" ([arXiv 2502.05791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05791)).

## mlkem-native is a real two-tool seam, and it changed three times in October

The program now has a concrete external target. **mlkem-native's CBMC proofs certify the C code (memory safety, type safety, no UB), and HOL Light with s2n-bignum certifies the AArch64/x86_64 assembly (functional correctness, memory safety, constant time).** At each C-to-assembly call site, the two meet through a contract that a human keeps in sync. In the project's own words: the assembly function "has both a HOL Light specification (proved against the object code) and a CBMC contract (assumed by CBMC when proving the C caller). These two specifications are written in different languages and are currently kept in sync by hand." The CBMC contract is "typically a subset of the HOL Light specification", and alignment constraints "cannot be faithfully expressed in CBMC's object model, and are currently omitted" ([SOUNDNESS.md](https://github.com/pq-code-package/mlkem-native/blob/main/SOUNDNESS.md)). The worked example is the AArch64 NTT. HOL Light proves that inputs bounded by 8191 give outputs bounded by 23594 and equal to `forward_ntt` mod 3329. The CBMC header requires a bound of 8192 and ensures 23595, and carries the comment `/* This must be kept in sync with the HOL-Light specification in proofs/hol_light/aarch64/proofs/mlkem_ntt_aarch64_asm.ml */` ([arith_native_aarch64.h](https://github.com/pq-code-package/mlkem-native/blob/main/mlkem/src/native/aarch64/src/arith_native_aarch64.h)). SOUNDNESS.md also lists each tool's trusted base, which in ledger terms are two very different entries. HOL Light has a ~400-line kernel that Candle can re-check. CBMC plus Z3/Bitwuzla plus the C preprocessor is "orders of magnitude larger" and has no independent proof checking. **The machine-checked HOL-Light-to-CBMC link is an open upstream issue, #1601.** That is the gap the project can close, or at least model honestly as a ledger entry that is never discharged.

The repo is changing this exact seam right now, which makes it a test of whether the composed claim tracks one side's change. On 2026-10-05 a commit dropped the alignment assumption on the x86_64 `tobytes`/`frombytes` buffers. On 2026-10-11 HOL Light stopped reporting incomplete proof runs as OK. On 2026-10-12 the README's CBMC claim was narrowed to exclude unproven backends ([commit log](https://github.com/pq-code-package/mlkem-native/commits/main)). The repo also contains semi-structured artifacts a parser can use: `subroutine_signatures.ml` lists per-function buffer sizes (effectively the ABI side of the seam), `s2n_bignum_allowlist.txt` lists permitted proof dependencies, and roughly 190 CBMC proof directories exist. Pairing each `kept in sync with ... .ml` comment with the `*_SUBROUTINE_CORRECT`/`*_SAFE` theorem in the named file could be automated. **Composing further downstream adds seams.** AWS-LC imported mlkem-native v2.0.0 on 2026-08-17 and pins the commit in `META.yml` ([META.yml](https://github.com/aws/aws-lc/blob/main/crypto/fipsmodule/ml_kem/META.yml)). It substitutes its own FIPS-202 behind glue headers, so a callee contract that CBMC assumed is now met by different code ([ml_kem README](https://github.com/aws/aws-lc/blob/main/crypto/fipsmodule/ml_kem/README.md)). That README calls the import "C-only", but the current tree contains native x86_64/aarch64 sources, so which is accurate needs checking before it is cited. **mldsa-native is a second instance of the same two-tool seam**, now that s2n-bignum has added x86 HOL Light proofs for seven ML-DSA routines ([s2n-bignum commits](https://github.com/awslabs/s2n-bignum/commits/main)).

verify-rust-std is a different case and it tests a recent result in this repo. Kani and ESBMC now check the *same* contracts in core: PR #710 on 2026-10-04 added checking contracts through ESBMC's native goto reader ([goto-transcoder.md](https://github.com/model-checking/verify-rust-std/blob/main/doc/src/tools/goto-transcoder.md)). Weekly JSON metrics are published ([PR #724](https://github.com/model-checking/verify-rust-std/pull/724)). This is redundancy, not composition. It is a live instance of the repo's newest finding that minimality composes across different tools for free but not for the same tool twice, and of whether two checkers sharing a CBMC GOTO front end count as independent. The metrics also need care. Of 16,748 std functions, 11,970 were verified against Kani's UB classes, but **only 295 were checked against contracts** ([arXiv 2606.17374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17374)). A ledger that treats "Kani verified" as functional correctness overstates by a factor of 40. libcrux has the most tools on paper but is the weakest target. Its Jasmin-wrapping and spec-equivalence sections are literal "TODO" text copied from the HACL* page ([libjade README](https://github.com/cryspen/libcrux/blob/main/formal_verification/libjade/README.md)), and its verification-status table has not changed since March ([verification_status.md](https://github.com/cryspen/libcrux/blob/main/libcrux-ml-kem/proofs/verification_status.md)).

What each tool emits decides how cheaply the project can ingest it. Only a few tools emit an assumption-bearing artifact at all:

| Tool | Consumable artifact | Carries assumptions? | Stability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lean | lean4export NDJSON; `lake check` / comparator verdict | axiom closure computable from export | format 3.1.0 versioned; verdict is pass/fail only |
| Dafny | `dafny audit --report-format md-table`, `--compare-report` | yes (axioms, `assume`, `{:verify false}`, bodyless ensures) | marked "under development"; self-diffing ([UserGuide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dafny-lang/dafny/master/docs/DafnyRef/UserGuide.md)) |
| SAW | `summarize_verification -f json` | yes (verified vs assumed per method; `admit` reasons) | documented "for consumption by other tools", unversioned ([CHANGES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GaloisInc/saw-script/master/CHANGES.md)) |
| Kani | `kani-list.json` | no (inventory; stubs and unwind bounds absent) | `file-version: "0.1"` ([output.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/model-checking/kani/main/kani-driver/src/list/output.rs)) |
| Verus | `--output-json` | no (counts and timing only) | unversioned |
| Jasmin 2026.09.0 | none new; adds "preliminary support for safety assertions" | possible new trusted item | ([releases](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/releases)) |
| CN | Lean CN_Lib obligations (added 2026-09-16) | obligations, not verdicts | no releases ([commits](https://github.com/rems-project/cn/commits/main)) |

Two rows bear directly on the existing pipeline. The project's Kani cross-check must keep deriving the trusted base (stubs, `stub_verified`, unwind bounds) from harness attributes, because Kani emits none of it. The `cross-check/leak-kani` capture approach is correct, not a workaround. SAW 1.6 (2026-09-17) deprecated `assume_unsat` in favour of `admit` with a reason string, and 1.4 added `--detect-vacuity`. Together these make SAW the most ledger-ready external emitter, and the natural third tool if the corpus needs one with honest assumption output.

## Attestation formats have no assumptions field, and the slot is unclaimed

**No supply-chain format has a first-class field for assumptions, and no one publishes formal-proof results as attestations.** The closest structural fit is in-toto SCAI v0.3. Each attribute has an `attribute`, an optional `target`, `evidence` (a ResourceDescriptor) and `conditions`, which is a free-form object for "conditions under which the attribute arises". Its `ATTESTED_DEPENDENCIES` pattern, which points `evidence` at a dependency's own attestation, is the only precedent for chaining ([SCAI spec](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/predicates/scai.md)). The spec asks only that semantics stay "consistent and monotonic". Union under composition and no silent discharge would have to live in the predicate's own semantics and in the project's verifier. CycloneDX 1.7 `declarations` (ECMA-424) is the richest claim/evidence/counterEvidence/confidence model, but it is designed for conformance against a standard's requirements and has no structural place for assumptions, so mapping into it loses information ([CycloneDX attestations](https://cyclonedx.org/use-cases/attestations); [ECMA-424](https://ecma-international.org/publications-and-standards/standards/ecma-424/)). SVR v0.2 (now requiring policies, merged 2026-07-17) and SLSA VSA are policy-gate summaries, not proof carriers ([SVR](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/main/spec/predicates/svr.md); [SLSA VSA](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/verification_summary)). SLSA has no track for artifact correctness.

Registration is slow and also unnecessary. **21 in-toto attestation PRs are open, mostly 2026 community predicates, none merged.** Many are AI-agent provenance and receipt types. Maintainer-driven changes took 1 to 9 weeks ([SPDX3 #508](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/pull/508); [SVR #555](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/pull/555)). Any TypeURI already works with cosign and GitHub attestations ([Sigstore docs](https://docs.sigstore.dev/cosign/verifying/attestation/)). So the practical sequence is: publish a ledger predicate under the project's own URI now, shaped as SCAI with a documented `conditions` sub-schema. Then open an ITE-9 PR that ships conformance vectors, which no current predicate has ([PR #597](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/pull/597)). Searches of the repo for "formal", "proof" and "lean" found no formal-verification predicate.

On the regulatory side, demand is real but indirect, and the dates have slipped. **CRA vulnerability reporting has applied since 11 September 2026**, but no harmonised standard is cited in the Official Journal. A draft amendment would push horizontal-standard drafts to 31 October 2026 and product-specific ones to 31 December 2026 ([cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-standardisation-deadlines-pushed-back-two-months.html)). NIST SSDF 1.2 is still a draft ([NIST SSDF](https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/ssdf/publications)). No regulator text found this round explicitly accepts machine-checked proofs as conformity evidence. The most concrete hook is VEX: a proof that a bug class is absent supports a `not_affected` justification such as `vulnerable_code_not_present`. This comes from model background and was not re-verified, so confirm it against the OpenVEX spec before relying on it. Since the EN 40000 generic-controls parts run to autumn 2027, there is still a window to get formal evidence mentioned in them. The August survey overstated how close the regulatory pull was. CRA asks for SBOMs and vulnerability handling, not proofs.

## What this implies for a standalone language and compiler

The notes contain no direct study of new security-language projects, so this section is inference from the evidence above and should be read that way. The previous survey's verdict still stands as the steelman against a new language. The IFC winners (Cocoon, Carapace, Filament) are libraries in Rust, and the field competes on false-positive rate, not on language design. A standalone language therefore has to justify itself by something a library cannot provide. The October evidence suggests what that is: **a compiler whose output is a certificate the shipped Lean kernels re-check, with every trusted item listed by machine.** EquiVM shows that foundational, compiler-independent end-to-end proofs at production scale are now feasible with LLM agents in Lean ([arXiv 2607.26306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26306)). Corten ([arXiv 2609.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04372)) and VerusBelt ([PLDI 2026](https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2026-pldi-verusbelt.pdf)) show that per-construct semantic soundness is how language-level verifiers now earn trust. SymCrypt's intrinsics policy is the template for the unavoidable residue: each hardware primitive gets either a proved model or "a deliberate trust decision" recorded as a Lean axiom, so `#print axioms` lists the trusted base mechanically ([INTRINSICS.md](https://github.com/microsoft/SymCrypt/blob/feature/verifiedcrypto/SymCRust/lean/Intrinsics/INTRINSICS.md)). CN adding Lean lemma export on 2026-09-16 shows established C tools converging on Lean as the obligation target ([CN commits](https://github.com/rems-project/cn/commits/main)).

Concretely, a language built on this repo should: emit its well-typedness and security-level certificates as lean4export NDJSON so `lake comparator --from-export --paranoid` checks them; put every FFI boundary, intrinsic and backend assumption into the computed `Ledger` as an axiom, never as prose; and treat the mlkem-native seam pattern as the interoperability problem it must solve on day one, since any real program will call verified assembly or verified C. The open risk is that the compiler itself becomes the "orders of magnitude larger" trusted base, which is CBMC's position in mlkem-native. The defence is translation validation into a Lean export, not proving the compiler.

## Conclusion

The two-month change reverses where the project's effort pays off. In August the scarce asset was writing Lean, and the trust story leaned on the project's own build. By late October **kernel re-checking is a toolchain feature, Lean proof is approaching commodity, and the exposed surfaces are the statement and the seam.** Both are surfaces the kernel cannot see, and the ledger was designed for them. The project's highest-value next work is not more algebra. It is three demonstrations that use these external inputs. First, ingest mlkem-native's CBMC/HOL Light seam as a ledger with the hand-sync obligation as an explicit entry that is never discharged, and show the composed claim change when the 2026-10-05 alignment fix lands. Second, reproduce the ePrint 2026/670 gap classes as ledger invariant violations. Third, emit the result as a SCAI-shaped attestation that five kernels re-checked.

The window is still open but narrower in a specific way. The likely competitor is not another composition algebra. It is a generalisation of Szeider's per-import trust table into a Lean library that unions trust-route tags, which is a weekend of work for any group that wants it. If the project publishes the mlkem-native case with earned omissions and minimality first, that library becomes a citation. If not, the project risks being described as that library's algebraic footnote. Two items need checking before they are relied on: whether AWS-LC's ML-KEM import is really C-only, and the exact output of `dafny audit` and SAW JSON. Both are quick to check, and both matter more than any further literature search.
