## Q1: Has anyone published machine-checked composition of per-artifact certificates from different tools with a computed trusted base since August 2026?

### Takeaway
I found none. Work since August 2026 does two things only: it imports certificates from one tool (SAT/LRAT, PB) into Lean and accounts for the trusted base per import mode, or it states trust assumptions for a single pipeline (Aeneas/SymCrypt, EquiVM) in prose. No source I found combines certificates from different tools into one algebraic object with a computed assumption union, minimality results, or bounded-result algebra. The narrow novelty claim still holds as far as these searches reach.

### Cited Findings
- **HDL (Teuber/Ulbrich/Platzer/Beckert)** appeared at PLDI 2026, pp. 1202–1227. Teuber's talk was dated 2026-06-19, and arXiv v2 was posted Apr 2026. I found no follow-up paper. Neighbouring work by the group is on hybrid/communicating programs, not certificate composition. — [Platzer pubs](https://symbolaris.com/pub/index.html); [Teuber talks](https://teuber.dev/talk/); [arXiv 2507.08581v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08581v2)
- **Loom line:** Velvet (CAV 2026, LNCS 16683; also Dafny'26) is a Loom-derived multi-modal verifier in Lean that unifies SMT, interactive proof, and PBT. "Certified Program Synthesis with a Multi-Modal Verifier" (arXiv 2604.16584) builds on Velvet for LLM pipelines. VCVio (ePrint 2026/899) builds on Loom for crypto. All of these are multiple modes inside one verifier. None composes after-the-fact certificates from external tools. — [Velvet CAV26](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-32526-6_11); [arXiv 2604.16584](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16584); [VCVio](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/899) [secondhand on VCVio's Loom dependency]
- **Streaming LRAT Certificates into Lean Theorems** (Szeider, arXiv 2607.00815; v1 2026-07-01, v2 2026-09-07). It turns SAT-solver LRAT into named Lean theorems through Lean core's verified checker, made resumable, and proves that a garbled input "can only fail the check but not yield a false theorem". Per the search summary, it tabulates how each import route enlarges the trusted base: Isabelle oracles, Rocq native_compute, a verified external checker, and Lean `by_decide` re-solving, which keeps compiler and runtime trusted. This is the closest new work on trusted-base accounting per certificate import, but it covers one tool class. — [arXiv 2607.00815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00815) [trust table is secondhand via search summary]
- **PBLean** (arXiv 2602.08692). The trusted base is the Lean kernel plus soundness lemmas for explicit proof terms. The `native_decide` path adds `Lean.trustCompiler`. This is a per-mode trust distinction, but static, not computed. — [arXiv 2602.08692](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08692) [secondhand]
- **EquiVM: Foundational Refinement Proofs for EVM Bytecode, at the Price of Tokens** (Lazaropoulos, Paraskevopoulou; arXiv 2607.26306; v2 2026-10-20). Lean EVM semantics plus the Stele spec language. LLM agents proved 35 production contracts end to end, independent of compiler. Per the search summary, it puts Lean's compiler and native evaluator in the trusted base and keeps agents outside it. It is single-tool, and the trust statement is prose. — [arXiv 2607.26306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26306)
- **Scaling Verification of Cryptographic Software with Aeneas, Rust, and Lean** (draft tech report, Sep 2026, arXiv 2609.15648). 237 KLOC of Lean covers 16.7 KLOC of Rust: safety, panic-freedom, and functional correctness for PQ suites on x86-64 and ARM. Per summaries, the trusted parts are the public-interface pre/postconditions, the faithfulness of Lean specs to NIST/IETF standards, and Charon/Aeneas extraction. Side channels are out of scope and handled by separate fuzzing and binary symbolic execution, i.e. evidence from another tool that is not composed formally. — [arXiv 2609.15648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15648); [MSR blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/verifying-rust-cryptography-in-symcrypt-from-standards-to-code/); critique: [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2609.15648) [secondhand]
- **Corten: Foundational Verification of Rust Programs** (Farka, Abate, Linker, Ertel; arXiv 2609.04372, 2026-09-03). Rocq plus Iris, with soundness built construct by construct against an interaction-trees denotation. It is single-framework, and the abstract has no trusted-base analysis. — [arXiv 2609.04372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04372)
- **Koomullil, Proof-Carrying Certificates for LLM Pipelines** (arXiv 2605.16407, May 2026). The validity check is a kernel type-check plus an axiom audit against a trusted set. Assumptions are tiered (math placeholders / crypto assumptions / ML-human oracles). It has 22 certificate types, and 17 of 46 audited declarations are axiom-free. I found no follow-up through Oct 2026. — [arXiv 2605.16407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16407)
- **Assurance 2.0:** A Springer chapter "Probabilistic Confidence in Assurance 2.0 Arguments" (book 978-3-032-34867-8, ch. 18) exists. It is probably the venue version of arXiv 2604.00034, but I could not confirm the venue or date. I found no new Bloomfield/Rushby paper on defeaters or composition after August. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-34867-8_18) [secondhand, unverified identity]
- **Certified Purity for Cognitive Workflow Executors** (arXiv 2605.01037) names a fixed TCB (WASM runtime, host functions, whitelist, gate, interpreter) and hash-chains certificates to a provenance ledger. "Ledger" here means provenance, not assumption union. — [arXiv 2605.01037](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01037)
- **Practitioner signals (GitHub, 2026):** lambdaclass/stabileo PR #334, "trusted computing base of the certificates, audited and enforced". eth-act/clifv issue #42, trusted-base tracking of `Lean.ofReduceBool`/`native_decide` in bv_decide certificates. teorth/IEANTN issue #164, whether a run "may depend on LeanCert". These show engineering demand for per-artifact axiom audits in Lean. They are ad hoc and not algebraic. — [stabileo #334](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/pull/334); [clifv #42](https://github.com/eth-act/clifv/issues/42); [IEANTN #164](https://github.com/teorth/IEANTN/issues/164) [secondhand, not opened]

### Inferences
- The field is converging on "import certificate into a Lean or Rocq kernel, then state the residual trusted base". The project's differentiator is unchanged: heterogeneous emitted certificates (Jasmin SCT levels, CN footprints, BMC/Alloy-scope) as composable objects, with the assumption set computed (union, no silent discharge, minimality, earned omissions) rather than written in prose.
- The nearest threat is a generalisation of Szeider's per-import trust table into a Lean library that tags each imported theorem with its trust route and unions the tags. That is small engineering and could appear quickly. The project could cite it and position itself as the algebra above it.
- The Loom/Velvet ecosystem is growing (Velvet, VCVio, synthesis). If Velvet adds external-certificate modes, it becomes a direct competitor. Watch verse-lab.

- I did not inspect the OOPSLA/SPLASH 2026, CCS 2026, CSF 2026, ITP 2026, FM 2026, ICFP 2026, SOSP 2026 or POPL 2027 accepted lists. The negative result covers only what web search surfaces.

- I did not check for follow-ups to arXiv 2510.10015 (verified/verifying compilation), Isabelle/SACM or ETB.

## Q2: Industrial practice: how multi-tool evidence and "what is assumed" are tracked

### Takeaway
Industry documents assumptions per tool and per proof, in slides, READMEs and paper sections, and never as a machine-checked combined object. AWS-LC is the clearest multi-tool case (SAW, an in-house AArch64 tool, Coq, HOL Light, CBMC), with assumptions listed per proof. Cryspen's hax is now under public attack for unrecorded trust boundaries. That is direct motivation for computed ledgers.

### Cited Findings
- **AWS-LC:** No single tool could handle the optimized code, so proofs combine SAW (C/x86_64 vs Cryptol), an in-house AArch64 tool, Coq (EC math) and HOL Light (s2n-bignum assembly). Slides mark some pieces as "assumed correct", e.g. P-384 field functions in SAW, and list a compiler/object-code assumption. The HOL Light s2n-bignum proofs carry no compiler assumptions. For ML-KEM (2026 talk), the C is checked for memory/type safety with CBMC and the assembly in HOL Light. I found no 2026 document that unifies the cross-tool assumptions. — [AWS-LC SoS 2024 slides](https://sos-vo.org/system/files/2024-05/Formal%20Verification%20of%20AWS-LibCrypto_1.pdf); [NIST AWS practices](https://www.nist.gov/document/formal-verification-cryptographic-software-aws-current-practices-and-future-trends); [ICMC 2026 session](https://icmconference.org/session/fast-formal-fips-cryptographic-implementations-in-aws-lc/) [secondhand via summaries]
- **Cryspen/libcrux/hax:** Kobeissi's "Verification Facade" (ePrint 2026/670, Apr 2026) analyses hax (Rust to F*) and names three gap classes: translation infidelity, unverified trust boundaries (operations axiomatized without postconditions), and specification gaming (escape hatches that inject unproven facts). It grades gaps as facade, conditional (depends on compilation mode) and scope. Five PoC exploits (ML-DSA, ML-KEM, Ed25519, ChaCha20) pass F* extraction without warnings. Per the search summary, a companion paper reports 13 libcrux vulnerabilities outside the verified code. Neither paper proposes a mechanism for tracking assumptions. — [ePrint 2026/670](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/670); [Cryspen response](https://cryspen.com/post/strengths-and-limitations/) [secondhand]
- hax 2026: the F* proof library is fully extracted from Rust core models (Jan 2026), and the Lean backend supports requires/ensures by Aug 2026. libcrux SIMD models carry Lean-only ensures and are excluded from portable hax extraction. A libcrux issue (Aug 2026) notes Lean extraction cfg-handling problems. So libcrux evidence is split across F* and Lean with different coverage. — [hax 2026 blog](https://hax.cryspen.com/blog/archive/2026/); [libcrux PR #1481](https://github.com/celabshq/libcrux/pull/1481); [issue #1575](https://github.com/celabshq/libcrux/issues/1575) [secondhand]
- **Jasmin in libcrux:** I found no source.
- **Microsoft SymCrypt:** see Q1. The trusted interface specs and extraction are disclosed in prose, and side-channel evidence comes from other tools and is not composed. — [arXiv 2609.15648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15648)
- **seL4 / Galois:** I found nothing new in this window. [gap]

### Inferences
- ePrint 2026/670's taxonomy maps almost one-to-one onto the project's ledger concepts. "Unverified trust boundary" is an assumption not in the ledger. "Specification gaming" is silent discharge. "Scope gap" is an omission that was not earned. "Conditional gap" is a configuration-indexed assumption. This is the strongest external motivation found. Cite it as the threat model that a computed ledger rules out by construction.
- AWS-LC's "assumed correct" boxes in slides are a hand-built version of the assumption union. It is a good concrete case study for "union under composition".

### Gaps
- I did not find aws-lc-verification or s2n-bignum README assumption sections for 2026, nor any Galois, seL4 or Cryspen document on cross-tool accounting.

## Q3: Multi-language and memory-model interoperability; BMC and completeness thresholds in assurance; algebraic confidence

### Takeaway
I found no 2026 paper that bridges the memory models of CN/Cerberus with Verus or Aeneas, or that treats BMC results as composable assurance evidence. That is an open space. Related work is single-tool soundness (VerusBelt, Corten) and C-to-Verus migration.

### Cited Findings
- VerusBelt (PLDI 2026) gives a semantic soundness foundation for a subset of Verus, covering safe and unsafe Rust. It is single-tool. — [VerusBelt PDF](https://iris-project.org/pdfs/2026-pldi-verusbelt.pdf)
- C2VR (Science of Computer Programming, 2026) migrates ACSL-annotated C to Verus-annotated Rust. Of 175 programs, 92 verified automatically and 139 after fixes. Two ACSL patterns could not be migrated. Spec translation across memory models is lossy, which is evidence that cross-tool spec correspondence is a real gap. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167642326001012)
- Completeness thresholds: I found no 2026 work. The foundational references are a compositional bound via recurrence diameter, not diameter (Abdulaziz/Gretton/Norrish ITP 2015, HOL4-mechanised), and the PSPACE-completeness of threshold linearity (Bundala/Ouaknine/Worrell 2012). — [TS abstract](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/nictaabstracts/Abdulaziz_GN_15_2.abstract); [AFP entry](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Factored_Transition_System_Bounding.html); [Bundala et al.](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~joel/publications/lct12abs.html)
- Monoidal/graded confidence algebras: I found no new 2026 source. Bloomfield/Rushby's probabilistic values are, per a critic, "only for comparative purposes". — [arXiv 2502.05791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05791)

### Inferences
- The Abdulaziz et al. result that diameter-based bounds do not compose but recurrence-diameter bounds do is directly relevant to "bounded certificates as composable objects". It gives a mechanised precedent for when a bounded result composes into an unbounded one. The project should cite it and check that its BMC-certificate algebra respects that distinction.
- Open problems the project could claim: (1) machine-checked cross-tool spec correspondence (CN footprint to Verus/Aeneas ownership), with the bridge itself as a ledger entry; (2) BMC-to-full lifting via mechanised completeness thresholds under composition; (3) turning ePrint 2026/670's gap taxonomy into ledger invariants; (4) per-import trust-route tags à la Szeider unified across tools.

### Gaps
- I did not search CN-specific 2026 work, Rust+C FFI verification, secure-compilation composition 2026, or graded-monad evidence papers, because of the tool-call budget.
