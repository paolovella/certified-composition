# The field, as of August 2026 — and where it is heading

*Written 2026-08-17. Companion to [`PLAN-2026-08.md`](PLAN-2026-08.md) §2, which was written
2026-08-14 from sources predating it. This is the refresh: what has moved, what is new, and
which of the three novelty claims in [`PLAN-MULTITOOL.md`](PLAN-MULTITOOL.md) §5 still
survive a literature search.*

**Method and its limits.** Web search plus abstract/PDF reads, one session. Items marked
*[secondhand]* come from search summaries whose primary source I did not open; treat their
numbers as approximate. The two most load-bearing claims (CompCert DO-178C qualification,
seL4 MCS on RISC-V) were confirmed against the vendor/project's own pages.

---

## 0. One-paragraph summary

The specialist layer got materially stronger in the last twelve months and — more
importantly — **crossed into certification and regulation**, which changes what a
composition layer is *for*. Meanwhile the composition layer itself, which
`PLAN-2026-08.md` §2.3 correctly described as thin, **has stopped being thin**: two 2026
papers (PLDI and POPL) now do machine-checked heterogeneous combination, one of them in
Lean. And a third force, absent from the August 14 assessment entirely, is now the loudest
thing in the field: **LLM-driven proof and specification generation**, which is
simultaneously the biggest threat to this project's cost model and the biggest source of
demand for exactly what it builds.

Net effect on the three claims:

| claim (`PLAN-MULTITOOL.md` §5) | status 2026-08-17 |
|---|---|
| (1) heterogeneous certificates accumulate by **proved laws** | **contested** — HDL (PLDI'26) and Loom (POPL'26) are now in this space. The claim survives only in its narrow form, and the narrowing must be written down. |
| (2) trusted base **computed from the artifact**, omissions earned | **still distinctive**, but the practice is being invented independently and informally. Window is closing. |
| (3) **bounded guarantees as first-class algebraic objects** | **still unoccupied.** No competitor found. But the *supply* of bounded certificates is moving out of software BMC and into hardware FV. |

---

## 1. The specialist layer: it professionalized

The August 14 table said "far behind, and not winnable." That verdict holds. What changed
is *why it matters*: the specialists now emit artifacts that regulators and certifiers
accept, which makes the certificate-consumption story a business problem rather than an
aesthetic one.

**CompCert is now a qualified tool in avionics.** AbsInt announced (press release
2026-03-20) that CompCert was qualified for the Multi-Function Computer New Generation of
the ATR 42/72, and that **certification credit under DO-178C / DO-330 / DO-333 was claimed
from compiler usage for the first time**. Source-level formal verification can now replace
specified object-code testing objectives. This is the single most consequential item on
this list: a formal-methods artifact was converted into a *regulatory credit* with a
Qualification Support Kit as the delivery mechanism. That kit — the packaging of evidence
plus its assumptions for an auditor — is the industrial-grade version of `Ledger`.

**seL4 verified its hardest configuration on a new architecture.** Proofcraft completed
functional correctness for **MCS seL4 on 64-bit RISC-V** (2026-06-29), shipped in seL4
16.0.0 (2026-07-22); Arm 64-bit port planned. MCS is the mixed-criticality scheduling
feature, i.e. the one that touches time. **Time protection remains out of the proofs** and
is still described by the Foundation as an open research problem — so row 2 of
`VULN-COVERAGE.md` (timing channels, IN-WITH-MODEL) is still describing something the
world's most verified kernel does not have.

**Jasmin/Formosa moved from papers to shipping post-quantum crypto.** `mlkem-libjade`
carries constant-time *and* speculative-constant-time via Jasmin's type system, with
functional correctness against a FIPS-203 transcription proved in EasyCrypt. Two
assumptions are stated and dated: the SHA-3 implementation's correctness, expected to be
discharged "during 2026", and dependence on unreleased Jasmin/EasyCrypt features. Note the
shape — **a deployed high-assurance artifact whose residual assumptions are published with
an expiry date.** That is the ledger discipline, by hand, in production, again.

**Rust verification became infrastructure.** The Kani paper (arXiv 2607.01504, ASE 2026
industry track) reports **>16,000 harnesses verified per code change** in the Rust standard
library campaign, plus production CI in Firecracker, s2n-quic, and Hifitime. The AWS/Rust
Foundation std-library effort reports 29 published challenges and 450+ PRs from 21+ external
contributors *[secondhand]*. Verus continues on the auto-active side.

**The direction of travel inside BMC matters for claim (3).** Kani's stated arc is
explicitly *bounded → unbounded*: function contracts, **loop contracts**, quantifiers, and
stubbing exist precisely to upgrade a bounded panic-freedom check into an unbounded
functional-correctness proof. Stage E already found the related fact — with unwinding
assertions on, an insufficient bound *fails* rather than silently returning a weaker
result. So the software BMC world is actively **eliminating** the bounded certificate rather
than learning to compose it. See §4.

**The empirical honesty is improving too.** "Do Unit Proofs Work?" (arXiv 2503.13762) is the
first empirical study of compositional BMC for memory safety: 73 unit proofs across four
embedded OSes, **74% of recreated defects detected, +9% with larger bounds, 19 new defects**,
~87 min to write and ~61 min to run per proof. That is the genre `corpus/RESULTS.md` belongs
to, and it is a useful calibration: the field's own honest numbers are not better than 8%
false rejection — they are differently shaped, and they are *measured*, which is the part
this repo already got right.

## 2. The composition layer: no longer empty

This is the section that most changes the plan.

**The Evidential Tool Bus is dead as software and alive as a research programme.** The ETB
repository's last change was 2015 *[secondhand]*. But Rushby did not stop: the live line is
**Assurance 2.0** (Bloomfield & Rushby), with tool support in **CLARISSA** (a DARPA project
led by Honeywell with Adelard and UT Dallas). The relevant new artifact is
**"Quantifying Confidence in Assurance 2.0 Arguments"** (Bloomfield & Rushby, arXiv
2604.00034, 2026-04-02), which takes on *exactly* the question stage F took on — what do you
do with evidence that is sound only within a scope? — and answers it with **Bayesian
confidence, graduated weights, and principled fusion of heterogeneous evidence**, together
with an explicit notion of *indefeasibility*, *defeaters*, and *eliminative argumentation*.

Read that carefully, because it is the strongest thing standing next to claim (3):

- They *do* address bounded/incomplete formal results, deliberately and recently.
- They address it by **quantifying confidence**, not by making the bound an algebraic
  object that composes. `bmul ⟨b₂,g₂⟩ ⟨b₁,g₁⟩ = ⟨min b₁ (b₂ - g₁), g₁ + g₂⟩` has no
  counterpart there, and `bounded_grade_does_not_factor` is a statement their framework
  cannot even phrase.
- So the gap in claim (3) is real — but it is now a gap *inside an actively developed
  competing answer*, not a gap in an empty field. The paper to cite-and-distinguish is this
  one, and `PLAN-MULTITOOL.md` §5 should be amended to name it. Also relevant: a scalability
  analysis of quantitative confidence methods for assurance cases (arXiv 2606.15480), which
  suggests the probabilistic route is hitting its own scaling walls.

**Two 2026 papers now do machine-checked heterogeneous combination.** This is the direct hit
on claim (1).

- **Heterogeneous Dynamic Logic: Provability Modulo Program Theories** — Teuber, Ulbrich,
  Platzer, Beckert; **PLDI 2026** (arXiv 2507.08581, v2 2026-04-14). Combines *distinct
  program logics* on the SMT/Nelson-Oppen model: individual dynamic logics become "dynamic
  theories" that can be combined. Two operations, **Lifting** and **Combination**;
  **soundness of all proof rules formalized in Isabelle**; case study verifies a Java
  controller against a differential-dynamic-logic plant model.
- **Foundational Multi-Modal Program Verifiers ("Loom")** — Gladshtein, Pîrlea, Zhao, Kurin,
  Sergey; **POPL 2026**. Monadic shallow embedding of executable semantics **into Lean**,
  with a library of monad transformers and — the theoretical core — **monad transformer
  algebras that automatically derive sound VC generators**. Two verifiers instantiated
  (distributed protocol safety; Dafny-style imperative specs). "Foundational" in their sense
  = proven sound in a general-purpose prover with a small trusted core.

**How claim (1) survives, and only how.** Neither paper does what this repo does, and the
difference is precise enough to be defensible:

| | HDL | Loom | here |
|---|---|---|---|
| object combined | proof *rules* of different logics | verifier *construction* modes | *certificates already emitted*, per artifact |
| when combination happens | during the proof | during verifier design | after the fact, over bytes another tool printed |
| what is accumulated | provability | soundness of VCs | **the guarantee's grade and its trusted base** |
| trusted base | not addressed | small trusted core (Lean kernel) | **computed per artifact, with earned-omission theorems** |

The honest statement is now: *heterogeneous combination is machine-checked in 2026 at the
level of logics and verifiers; it is not machine-checked at the level of emitted
per-artifact certificates with an accumulating trusted base.* That is a narrower claim than
§5 currently makes, and §5 should be rewritten to it before anyone else notices the
difference. Adjacent and worth reading for the same reason: **"End-to-end Compositional
Verification of Program Safety through Verified and Verifying Compilation"** (arXiv
2510.10015), whose *open safety* is a notion of safety **composable at the target level**
across heterogeneous safe/unsafe modules — the closest thing to stage C's `hmap` problem
solved for one property, and "Unifying Compositional Verification and Certified
Compilation" (POPL 2025).

**Proof interoperability is a mature, separate stack that this repo has been ignoring.**
Alethe (generic SMT proof format, reconstructible in Coq and Isabelle), Dedukti/Lambdapi as
the λΠ-modulo pivot, EuroProofNet, and the Inria CARMA associate team are an established
community whose entire job is moving proofs between systems. This is *not* a competitor —
they translate proofs, this repo accumulates certificates *without* translating them — but
it is the obvious place a reviewer will ask "why not just translate?", and the answer
(translation requires a common logic; Jasmin's SCT levels and CN's footprints have none)
should be written down once, with a citation.

**Assurance-case tooling with formal proof inside it exists.** Isabelle/SACM integrates
machine-checked proof into OMG SACM assurance cases with well-formedness and traceability
guarantees; a continuous-assurance framework spanning design/runtime/evolution time was
posted 2025-11 (arXiv 2511.14805) — with only the design-time phase actually built. The
field's answer to "many kinds of evidence, one argument" is still overwhelmingly
*structured argument + confidence*, not *algebra*.

## 3. The thing that was not in the August 14 assessment at all: AI

This is the largest single change in the field's shape and it is barely a year old.

- **Vericoding as a named discipline.** The benchmark (arXiv 2509.22908, POPL 2026 Dafny
  workshop) ships **12,504 formal specifications** — 3,029 Dafny, 2,334 Verus/Rust, 7,141
  Lean — and off-the-shelf LLM success rates of **82% Dafny, 44% Verus/Rust, 27% Lean**,
  with pure Dafny verification reported moving **68% → 96% in a year**. Adding
  natural-language descriptions did *not* help, which is the interesting finding: the
  specification is doing the work.
- **Proof automation reached systems-scale ITP.** Neuro-symbolic best-first proof search
  with LLM step proposal and symbolic repair/ranking proves **up to 77.6% of seL4 theorems**
  (arXiv 2603.19715, OSDI 2026), beating standalone Sledgehammer and prior LLM approaches on
  multi-step proofs. Against a project that cost ~20 person-years and 100K+ lines of proof
  script.
- **Specification generation is now the bottleneck being attacked.** Verus-SpecGym (arXiv
  2605.26457) is an agentic environment for evaluating *specification* autoformalization.
  NeurIPS 2026 has a full-day **VeriCodeGen** workshop on agents co-designing specs, code
  and proofs with machine-checkable guarantees.
- **Money and institutional weight moved.** Amazon's automated-reasoning group made the
  largest donation in Lean FRO history (July 2026); Microsoft Research verifies production
  SymCrypt crypto in Lean via Aeneas; AWS shipped **Automated Reasoning checks** in Bedrock
  Guardrails to GA (2025-08) with formal policy checking of LLM output and customers in six
  industries *[secondhand]*; Ethereum Foundation funds zkVM verification in Lean.
- **DARPA PROVERS** (42 months, three phases) exists specifically to make formal methods
  usable by non-experts inside an ordinary CI pipeline, built on PEARLS' proof
  repair/learning results.

**What this means for this project, bluntly.** Two opposite pressures:

1. *Threat to the moat.* "Writing Lean is expensive" was an implicit barrier protecting a
   solo research programme. It is eroding fast. A well-resourced group can now formalize a
   composition algebra in weeks. The defensible asset is not the Lean; it is **the
   question**, the corpus, and the negative results (`min_unsound`,
   `bounded_grade_does_not_factor`, `ni_and_memory_do_not_factor`,
   `sequential_model_misses_races`) — findings that took adversarial taste to produce, which
   is the part that does not automate yet.
2. *Enormous new demand for the deliverable.* Agent-written code at scale creates, for the
   first time, a mass population of artifacts that are (a) cheap to produce, (b) untrusted,
   and (c) accompanied by machine-checkable evidence of wildly heterogeneous strength — a
   Kani harness here, a Verus contract there, an LLM-judge score, a test suite, a type
   system. **Deciding what a pile of mixed-strength evidence about one artifact adds up to
   is about to be a routine industrial problem.** That is this repo's exact object. Note
   that arXiv 2605.16407 ("Proof-Carrying Certificates for LLM Pipelines", Koomullil, May
   2026) already does a crude version — Lean 4 kernel type-check plus **transitive axiom
   audit against {propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}**, assumptions partitioned into
   tiers (mathematical placeholders / cryptographic / ML-human oracles), 46 declarations
   audited, 17 axiom-free. **That is `Ledger` and `needs`, hand-rolled, by someone else, for
   LLM pipelines, three months ago.** Claim (2)'s window is not closed, but it is visibly
   closing, and the people arriving at it are arriving from the AI side.

## 4. Regulation is now the demand-side driver

`VULN-COVERAGE.md` and `LedgerBridge` were built as intellectual objects. The market for
them is being created by law.

- **EU Cyber Resilience Act.** Obligation to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and
  severe incidents starts **11 September 2026** — three weeks from now; full compliance
  December 2027. Manufacturers must produce and maintain an **SBOM in a machine-readable
  format**, and — the part that matters here — the SBOM is **part of the product's official
  technical file**, producible on demand to market-surveillance authorities *[secondhand,
  vendor sources]*.
- **CISA memory-safety roadmaps** were expected by **1 January 2026** for existing products
  in memory-unsafe languages: inventory, new-development plan, migration strategy, timeline
  — or hardware capability (CHERI) as the alternative *[secondhand]*.
- **DO-178C credit from formal methods** is now demonstrated (§1), which sets a precedent
  other domains will cite.

The through-line: **regulators are asking for a machine-readable statement of what a
product is made of and what is known about it.** SBOM is that statement for *composition*;
it says nothing about *guarantees*. `supply_chain_ledger` and `ledgerBridge_comp_ledger` are
a proposal for the missing half — an SBOM whose entries carry what was proved and what was
assumed, accumulating by a proved law. The standards to attach to are **in-toto
attestations (ITE-6)** and **SLSA provenance v1.2**: a signed statement binding a predicate
to a subject artifact, already the industry substrate. A `Ledger` serialized as an in-toto
predicate is a small, concrete, and unusually well-timed piece of work.

## 5. IFC: the graveyard verdict was right, and the survivors show the shape

`PLAN-2026-08.md` §2.4 said retrofitting had replaced language-building. That is now
emphatic:

- **Cocoon** (PLDI/OOPSLA'24) — static IFC as a *library* in unmodified Rust with the
  unmodified compiler; evaluated by retrofitting a Spotify client and Servo.
- **Carapace** (OOPSLA'25) — Cocoon's approach plus dynamic IFC, secrecy *and* integrity,
  "relatively few changes" to retrofit, negligible runtime overhead in most cases.
- **Filament** (arXiv 2604.14357, April 2026) — Denning-style, *variable-level* precision,
  no compiler modification, implicit flows via a `pc_block!` macro, library interop via
  `fcall!`/`mcall!`, and explicitly **more permissive than Cocoon** with fewer escape
  hatches.

Three papers in three years, each one buying permissiveness back. The trajectory is
unambiguous: **the winning form factor is a library in a language people already use, and
the competitive axis is false-positive rate.** The freeze on `UCmd` in
`PLAN-MULTITOOL.md` §3 is the right call and this is the evidence for it. It also means the
17%-inexpressible / 8%-false-rejection numbers in `corpus/RESULTS.md` are measuring the axis
the field competes on — which makes them worth *more* than the repo currently claims, and
also means they will never be competitive for a bespoke language.

## 6. Where the field is going — six bets

1. **Evidence packaging becomes a product category.** Qualification Support Kits, SBOM+
   attestation, assurance-case tooling and CI-attached proof artifacts converge on "a signed
   machine-readable bundle of what is known about this artifact." *(High confidence — three
   independent drivers: CRA, DO-178C precedent, SLSA/in-toto adoption.)*
2. **The bottleneck moves from proof to specification.** Vericoding results already show
   spec quality dominating; Verus-SpecGym and VeriCodeGen are the field organizing around
   it. Expect "who wrote the spec, and against what model" to become the central trust
   question — which is exactly the `hmap` problem stage C isolated. *(High confidence.)*
3. **Bounded results retreat in software and persist in hardware.** Kani's contracts, loop
   contracts and completeness-threshold work push software toward unbounded claims;
   industrial hardware FV still lives on bounded proofs and manual decomposition to raise
   bounds. If claim (3) wants real instances, **hardware formal verification is where the
   bounded certificates actually are.** *(Medium confidence — worth one afternoon of
   checking, e.g. the ISCA 2026 FAVA tutorial materials, before betting a stage on it.)*
4. **Heterogeneous combination gets crowded from the logic side.** HDL and Loom are the
   first two; SMT-style modular combination is a template everyone knows. Expect more, and
   expect them not to touch trusted-base accounting — that is a systems/assurance concern
   and those authors are PL people. *(Medium-high confidence.)*
5. **Assurance 2.0 vs. an algebra becomes a real disagreement.** Bloomfield & Rushby are
   actively publishing the probabilistic answer with DARPA-funded tooling. Anyone proposing
   an algebraic answer will be asked why, and "because confidence does not compose
   associatively" needs to be a theorem, not a preference. *(High confidence that the
   question gets asked.)*
6. **AI-generated proof makes formalization cheap and taste expensive.** 77.6% of seL4
   theorems, 96% on Dafny verification tasks. The scarce input becomes knowing *which*
   theorem to state and *which* counterexample to hunt. *(High confidence.)*

## 7. Consequences for this repo, in its own terms

Not a new plan — five things §2–§6 imply, each falsifiable.

1. **Rewrite `PLAN-MULTITOOL.md` §5.** It currently claims (1) exists "here already" against
   a field where HDL and Loom now stand. The claim is still defensible in its narrow form —
   *emitted per-artifact certificates, accumulated after the fact, with a computed trusted
   base* — but the narrowing has to be explicit and the two papers cited. Doing this
   unprompted is worth more than any new Lean, because the alternative is a reviewer doing
   it for you.
2. **Claim (3) needs its competitor named and its instances relocated.** Cite Bloomfield &
   Rushby 2604.00034 and state the difference in one paragraph: they quantify confidence in
   a scope-limited result; `BoundedCert` composes the scope. Then go find bounded
   certificates that are *not* going away — hardware FV, Alloy scopes, TLC finite instances
   — because Kani's are being contract-ed out of existence.
3. **Claim (2) is the one with a closing window and the clearest external pull.** The
   Koomullil paper hand-rolls a tiered axiom ledger; CRA makes machine-readable
   what's-inside-this mandatory in three weeks; in-toto/SLSA is the substrate everyone
   already signs. A `Ledger` emitted as an in-toto predicate — with
   `ledger_no_silent_discharge` as the property the format enforces — is small, novel, and
   currently unclaimed.
4. **The TLC probability gap found in stage E is now a named research area, not an
   oddity.** "`Ledger = List Prop` has nowhere to put a probability" is precisely where the
   algebraic and probabilistic answers meet. Bloomfield & Rushby's Bayesian fusion is the
   other side of that seam. This is the most interesting open technical question the repo
   has stumbled on and it is currently recorded as a footnote.
5. **The ω₁ line is untouched by all of this**, as `PLAN-2026-08.md` already says. Searches
   for 2025–26 work on ρ₃/coherent sequences turned up the higher-dimensional walks line
   (arXiv 2410.00607) and the cohomology-of-ordinals programme, nothing bearing on
   dip-schedulability. `PROBLEM-dip-schedulability.md` remains correctly aimed at set
   theorists rather than at this field.
